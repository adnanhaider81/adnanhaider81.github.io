How I make pathogen-genomics workflows traceable: from sample sheet to public-health report

A sequencing workflow is not only a set of commands. A useful public-health workflow connects each result to the run, barcode or index, control review, analysis version, QC decision, interpretation status, and reporting decision.

Traceability QC Reproducibility

Why traceability matters

Pathogen-genomics results are often used in surveillance contexts where the evidence chain matters as much as the final sequence. A result is stronger when a reviewer can see which sample or synthetic public-safe record was analyzed, which run it belonged to, which controls were reviewed, which workflow version was used, and whether the final output was reportable, repeated, held for manual review, or not reportable.

The evidence chain I use

  1. Sample sheet or synthetic public-safe sample record.
  2. Library barcode or index map.
  3. Sequencing run and run-level QC summary.
  4. Pipeline version, environment, and command record.
  5. Control review and contamination-review notes.
  6. Consensus, genotype, lineage, or target-region status.
  7. Reportability decision and handover status.

Turning internal quality work into public evidence

Internal quality-system records often cannot be shared publicly. The structure of that work can still be made visible through public-safe templates: sample-to-report traceability tables, run-review logs, contamination and repeat-run checklists, workflow verification summaries, release checklists, Zenodo metadata, and protocols.io-ready checklist records.

What I do not publish

I do not publish restricted raw sequencing data, patient identifiers, exact surveillance sites, internal sample IDs, confidential reports, credentials, or unapproved wet-lab operating details. Public examples use synthetic data and broad categories only.
